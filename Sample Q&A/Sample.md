### Money-Saving Tips for Your Family Trip
1. **Use SimplyGo (Knowledge Base):** Do not buy standard single-trip tickets or expensive tourist passes. Simply tap your international contactless Visa/Mastercard credit or debit cards at the MRT gantry. It only costs about $1.00–$2.50 per trip (*Source: Wikivoyage: Singapore Travel Guide*).
2. **Eat at Hawker Centres (Knowledge Base):** Singapore's hawker culture is UNESCO-recognized and incredibly cheap. You can get world-class meals like Hainanese Chicken Rice or Laksa for under 5 SGD (~376 INR) per plate (*Source: Wikivoyage: Singapore Travel Guide*).
3. **Bring Water Bottles:** Tap water in Singapore is perfectly safe to drink (*Source: Visit Singapore: Essential Travel Information*). Bring reusable bottles to save on buying bottled water at attractions, where prices are marked up.

## Interaction 5
**User:** What are the most visited places in singapore?

**Assistant:** Based on travel data and visitor popularity, here are the most visited and iconic places in Singapore:

### 1. Gardens by the Bay
This futuristic, 101-hectare park in the Marina district is Singapore's most famous landmark. It is home to the iconic **Supertree Grove** (which lights up spectacularly at night), the **Flower Dome** (the world's largest glass greenhouse), and the **Cloud Forest**, which features a stunning 35-meter-tall indoor waterfall.
* *Source:* [Wikivoyage: Singapore Travel Guide](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Singapore)

### 2. Marina Bay Sands & The Merlion Park
No trip to Singapore is complete without visiting the Marina Bay waterfront. You can walk around the entire perimeter of the bay, take a photo with the iconic half-lion, half-fish **Merlion statue**, and admire the architectural marvel of the **Marina Bay Sands** resort. At night, the bay comes alive with the free *Spectra* light and water show.
* *Source:* [Wikivoyage: Singapore Travel Guide](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Singapore)

### 3. Sentosa Island
Known as the "State of Fun," Sentosa is a massive island resort dedicated to leisure and entertainment. It features three sandy beaches (Siloso, Palawan, and Tanjong), **Universal Studios Singapore**, **Adventure Cove Waterpark**, and the **S.E.A. Aquarium** (one of the largest aquariums in the world).
* *Source:* [Wikivoyage: Singapore Travel Guide](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Singapore)

### 4. Mandai Wildlife Reserve
For nature and animal lovers, this world-class reserve is a must-visit. It houses four distinct wildlife parks:
* **Singapore Zoo:** Famous for its "open-concept" rainforest habitats.
* **Night Safari:** The world's first nocturnal zoo.
* **River Wonders:** Asia's first and only river-themed wildlife park.
* **Bird Paradise:** A newly opened, state-of-the-art bird sanctuary.
* *Source:* [Wikivoyage: Singapore Travel Guide](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Singapore)

### 5. Singapore Botanic Gardens
Established in 1859, this 82-hectare tropical garden is Singapore's first and only **UNESCO World Heritage Site**. It is highly popular for family picnics, morning jogs, and features the breathtaking **National Orchid Garden**, which showcases thousands of orchid species.
* *Source:* [Wikivoyage: Singapore Travel Guide](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Singapore)

### 6. Historic Cultural Enclaves
To experience Singapore's rich multicultural heritage, visitors flock to these vibrant neighborhoods:
* **Chinatown:** Home to traditional shophouses, the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple, and incredible street food.
* **Little India:** A bustling, colorful sensory experience filled with spice shops, temples, and the 24-hour Mustafa Centre.
* **Kampong Glam (Malay-Arab Quarter):** Known for the majestic Masjid Sultan, trendy boutiques along Haji Lane, and delicious Middle Eastern and Malay cuisine.
* *Source:* [Wikivoyage: Singapore Travel Guide](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Singapore)

### 7. Orchard Road
This is Singapore's ultimate shopping belt, stretching over 2 kilometers. It is lined with dozens of mega-malls, luxury flagship stores, and endless dining options, making it a paradise for shoppers.
* *Source:* [Wikivoyage: Singapore Travel Guide](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Singapore)

### 1. Vegetarian Bee Hoon (斋米粉)
* **What it is:** A classic Singaporean staple. It consists of flavorful, stir-fried thin rice vermicelli (bee hoon) topped with a variety of mock meats (made from soy or wheat gluten), crispy fried beerkwan (soy sheets), and braised vegetables. 
* **Why try it:** It is incredibly savory, filling, and very cheap.
* **Estimated Cost:** **SGD 3.50 to SGD 5.00** (~**INR 262 to INR 375**) per plate.

### 2. Vegetarian Yong Tau Foo (酿豆腐)
* **What it is:** A fun, interactive dish where you get to choose your own ingredients from a display counter (*Source: Wikivoyage*). For a vegetarian version, select items like fresh tofu, fried tofu puffs, mushrooms, bitter gourd, lady’s finger (okra), and seaweed. These are boiled on the spot and served either in a clear vegetable broth or dry with sweet sweet-bean sauce and chili.
* **Why try it:** It is healthy, customizable, and comforting—especially if there is a light drizzle outside!
* **Estimated Cost:** **SGD 5.00 to SGD 7.00** (~**INR 375 to INR 525**) depending on how many pieces you select.

### 3. Vegetarian Economy Rice (菜饭 - Cai Fan)
* **What it is:** You start with a plate of steamed white or brown rice and point to choose 2 to 3 vegetable or tofu side dishes from the display (*Source: Wikivoyage*). Look for the dedicated Chinese Vegetarian stalls at Maxwell. You can choose delicious options like braised eggplant, stir-fried broccoli, curry vegetables, or sweet-and-sour mock meats.
* **Why try it:** It is the ultimate local working-class meal—fast, cheap, and highly nutritious.
* **Estimated Cost:** **SGD 4.00 to SGD 6.00** (~**INR 300 to INR 450**) per plate.
